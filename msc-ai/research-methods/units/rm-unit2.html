Research Methods - Unit 2: Research Questions, the Literature Review and the Research Proposal

Overview

As per the course website, "Welcome to Week 2, where you will be introduced to the various aspects of a research proposal, starting with defining a research question. Defining the topic of your research may be a tedious process, and one that appears often ‘daunting’ (Golash-Boza, 2016), but it is essential to ensure you are successful in your research. You will start with an initial research idea and then ask pertinent questions that may need further revisions before you prepare your research proposal, which is the foundation to your research."

Reading Reflection

The reading list included several items, all related to literature review. The most prominent reading was Chapter 14 of a book named Writing about Learning and Teaching in Higher Education by Healey, Matthews and Cook‐Sather (2020), which comprised several insights from several researchers on the matter, including -for example- the six-charactertistic taxonomy by Harris Cooper on the nature and purpose of literature reviews, regarding focus, goal, perspective, courage, organisation and audience. The chapter also added 12 purposes of literature reviews that were identified by Chris Hart, from distinguishing what has been done from what needs to be done, to understanding the origins and structure of the subject, to having a body of knowledge to which you can relate your own research findings. In addition, the chapter mentioned standards and criteria for reviews as stated by the Review of Educational Research (RER) Journal that ranged from quality of the literature, quality of analysis, significance of the topic, impact of the article, advancement of the field, style, balance and fairness and purpose. The chapter then synthesises all the previous into eight guiding questions for planning, revising and refining a literature review:

  1. What is the focus and aim of your review? Who is your audience?
  2. Why is there a need for your review? Why is it significant?
  3. What is the context of the topic or issue? What perspective do you take? What framework do you use to synthesize the literature?
  4. How did you locate and select sources for inclusion in the review?
  5. How is your review structured?
  6. What are the main findings in the literature on this topic?
  7. What are the main strengths and limitations of this literature?
  8. What conclusions do your draw from the review? What do you argue needs to be done as an outcome of the review?

There was also a chapter from another book titled Handbook of eHealth Evaluation: An Evidence-based Approach by Paré and Kitsiou (2017) that included a typology of research reviews that included seven types, as well as a paper on artificial intelligence and the conduct of literature reviews by Wagner, Lukyanenko and Paré (2021).

Overall the readings, as well as the seminar, introduced several considerations that will be useful to take care of when I'll start working on my literature review, which should be soon, right after I pick up on the weeks that I've missed.
